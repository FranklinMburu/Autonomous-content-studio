## Maitis: how a video is built (KscFyux4dVk 0:00–3:00 and 10:00–11:30; qKsMQg4WB6s 0:00–2:30)

- **Presenter on screen only 10–17% of the time**, in 2–5 s inserts (0:18, 0:29, 0:40, 1:50–2:05 …). The rest is
  motion graphics and screen recordings.
- **The presenter:** a woman with curly hair, grey hoodie, cozy living room (fireplace, landscape painting, a visible
  softbox light), purple rim light added in post. Natural hand gestures and expressions; consistent identity across
  frames. The channel says it is "AI-powered"; the stills alone can't prove she is an AI avatar, but the short-insert
  pattern is exactly how avatar channels hide weak spots. To verify: lip-sync close-ups over consecutive frames
  (next step).
- **Hook (0–20 s):** a story, not a pitch: "When Apple launched the iPhone in 2007 / Google was secretly building
  [an old keyboard phone] / they took one look at the iPhone / studied how it worked / rebuilt around a touchscreen /
  Android runs most of the phones on the planet / maybe even the one in your hand" → then the turn to the topic
  ("Google never needed Apple's code… understood it, then built their own").
- **Text:** the narration is shown as **headline text built word by word**, white with keywords in the brand purple,
  sometimes underlined. Not subtitles at the bottom: it IS the visual.
- **Look:** near-black and deep navy with a purple glow (brand colour); clean sans-serif; rounded dark cards;
  3D-tilted UI panels with depth of field and slow camera dollies (the 10-minute section moves to a teal/cyan
  accent: one accent per chapter); real app logos and real screenshots (YouTube, Google, DeepSeek); diagrams that
  build (a 3-level pyramid "Set up → Connect → Build"; Apple → eye → own phone).
- **Pacing:** scene-detector cuts 2.7–6.8 per minute, but nearly every second has motion (words appearing, panels
  sliding, camera drifting), so it never feels still.
- **Retention devices:** an open loop ("I've saved the biggest SURPRISE for the end" at 1:55), "Here's the plan",
  a promise of value (free, $0, 760+ tools), a comments prompt, a subscribe pill.
- **Sound:** -16.1 to -16.9 LUFS integrated, loudness range 2.5–3.9 LU (even, voice-first).
- **Funnel:** every video points to maitis.ai (prompts, commands, links); the description says "every command, prompt
  and link from this video".

## Griffin Wooldridge: how a video is built (Iup1WlUyj9M 0:00–3:00; zsCGS-Yzjnw 0:00–2:00)

- **A real person, on camera at the start** (the first 28 s almost entirely), then about 7–20% of the time.
- **Look:** cinematic and moody: dark room, warm salt lamp, shallow depth of field, a soft key light; black T-shirt.
  Brand moments: a deep-blue gradient wave; clean white title cards ("frontend", "implement design").
- **On-camera graphics:** keywords and logos pop in beside his head as he says them ("Claude", "Skills"), small UI
  elements (a menu, "Saved!", "Error", toggles) float next to him.
- **Body:** real screen recordings (Claude Code, GitHub, Figma, the finished site), zoomed and cropped to what
  matters; component close-ups on black; side-by-side comparisons of the same UI made three ways.
- **No subtitles**; text appears only as labels and titles.
- **Pacing:** 4.5–9 scene cuts per minute; average shot 6.7–13 s.
- **Sound:** exactly -14.0 LUFS, loudness range 1.2–3.6 LU (broadcast-style consistency).

## What this means for us (facts → choices)

1. Both channels are **demo-first**: the presenter is a frame, not the content. Maitis's presenter is on screen 10–17%
   of the time. So an avatar is optional at first; the motion graphics and real demos carry the video.
2. **Our TE motion engine already speaks Maitis's visual language** (dark base, one accent, 3D panels, camera moves,
   word-synced headline text, link-and-pop). The long-form lane extends it rather than starting over: chapters with
   their own accent, real screenshots and screen recordings, a story hook, open loops, a resource page per video.
3. **Titles and topics:** Maitis wins on "FREE alternative to paid X / China gives it away"; Griffin on "tool X for
   designers / ban the AI look". Our honest angle: a $0 builder showing a real working system.
4. **Loudness target for long-form:** -14 to -16 LUFS, range under 4 LU.
5. **Measured on 13 Nov (afternoon):**
   - **Speech** (Whisper on the sections): Maitis 153–176 words a minute, 20–26 pauses a minute (median 0.56 s),
     8.5–12 words per sentence: a calm, pause-rich storyteller. Griffin 208–224 words a minute, ~15 pauses a minute
     (0.42–0.46 s), 13–17 words per sentence: fast and conversational. Our TE voice (~155 wpm) sits at Maitis' pace.
   - **Maitis presenter, 16 consecutive frames at 25 fps (0:39):** natural mouth shapes (pucker, open, teeth, smile),
     sharp teeth identical frame to frame, stable freckles, natural squints: no lip-sync artefacts. Either a real person
     or an avatar beyond any open model we can run at $0. Our plan stands: a real consenting person filmed once, short
     MuseTalk inserts.
   - **Still to measure:** the full retention structure of one whole video (chapters, re-hooks).
6. **Built from this (PR #107):** the `headline_build` scene (the spoken line builds word by word as a big headline,
   keywords in the accent colour), and the tech radar, which reads these two channels (and 20 more) every run to see
   which video is beating its usual views right now.
